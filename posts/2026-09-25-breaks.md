A long gap on writing here.  In March 2026 I developed a [Pneumococcal infection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumococcal_infection) which lead to being hospitalised for 3 weeks with [Bacterial Meningitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meningitis) amongst other things.  I was off work until recently.

I was supported by a huge number of doctors, nurses, and other people working in the [NHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Health_Service).  I can't thank them enough.  Since returning to work, my employer, and especially the pure maths section and colleagues, have been very understanding.

<!--more-->

I dithered over whether to write more about this experience of a life-threatening illness.  At least in my experience, in the UK, we don't really talk about such things.  I think our society would be better if we discussed such things openly; I also worried that I'd come across as weird if I did write more.  So I haven't.  Except, I think our profession would be better if we treated each other more as humans, and less as theorem-producing machines.

Which, of course, brings me to a second "break".  I paid no attention to anything happening externally during the 3 weeks I was in hospital, and then took the somewhat deliberate decision to not engage with news, social media etc. once I was home.  (I'm pretty "offline" anyway.  I just listened to the BBC radio news for a few months, instead of reading a news website etc.)  I didn't engage with Mathematics except a little to keep my mind active (and to check I could still do abstract maths: thankfully nothing much seems to have changed there.)

I was hence rather late to learn about what was happening over the summer.  This lead to a pretty surreal week where it rather felt like reality had changed.  I went back to work and there was one and only one topic of conversation.  I'm somewhat doom scrolling [Proofs and Prompts](https://proofsandprompts.com/) and [Terence Tao's blog](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/).  One blog I like is [Michael Harris's](https://siliconreckoner.substack.com) (to get a sense of the pace of change, read the "about" page...)

Again, I have nothing profound to add.  A Mathematician is a machine for turning coffee into theorems.  A data-centre doesn't run on coffee.  Neither do I anymore: more than one cup and I feel pretty unwell...
